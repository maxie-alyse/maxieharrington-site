Case Study — Operations · 2024–

Building Story.

A film studio doesn't scale on taste alone. This is the part of the work that doesn't end up on screen — growing Story Co. from a handful of people into a full studio with a sister company.

01 — 2024

A founder and a small, talented team.

I joined Story on day one, when it was built mostly on vision, grit, and talent. The first job was making the studio real underneath the work — contracts, finance, hiring, production infrastructure — so that every new film started from solid ground instead of from scratch.

02 — The machine

Systems that let creatives create.

Hiring, procurement, immigration, client delivery — processes that run without being chased. Ideas, gear, teams — organized before anyone has to ask. The point of operations at a studio is simple: every problem solved off screen shows up on screen.

03 — Premieres

Proof, in theater seats.

Four premieres, 2,200+ seats — including a sold-out 960 at the Palace of Fine Arts for Planet. Venue, ticketing, run-of-show, merch, crowd: produced end to end, in house — including the social campaigns that filled the rooms. Each premiere is built as an experience, not just a screening — made with Bay Area crews and local talent, for an audience that keeps showing up. Movies were always meant to be watched together. We build the rooms for it.

04 — 2026

A studio, a sister company, and counting.

Growth that compounds instead of bloats: The Greatest Lie was made for a quarter of the time and budget of Planet — deliberately. That's the foundation for what's next: shows and films that reach large audiences and still land close to home — stories that create empathy, offer hope, and make someone's crazy idea feel worth chasing. Some of those ideas will change the world for the better.

Operations is a creative discipline.
